# Jet approach and camera references

Research date: 4 October 2026. Purpose: refine the SR-71 intro's shot sequence, especially route B: a small aircraft low in an expansive sky, an approach that gathers speed, a camera retreat/rise, then a move into the poster's side profile. Style is secondary to composition.

## What was checked

All 20 numbered links and two bonus links below opened and played in the YouTube browser player. Selected frames were visually inspected and the visible player clock was used for approximate review ranges; these are **not frame-accurate edit points**, and the videos were not watched end to end. Observations describe visible frames. “Borrow” describes our design interpretation, not a claim about the filmmaker's intent or how a shot was technically made. The two unnumbered bonus entries are explicitly adjacent references rather than distant approaches. No footage was downloaded and no paid generation was used.

Titles are source labels, not verification of claims such as “lowest ever,” speed, G force, or sonic booms. Different uploads can show related airshows, but each entry is a distinct clip/viewpoint. Amateur tracking and zoom can change apparent growth; don't assume that every size change is physical camera travel.

## Start with these five

- **#1, F-18 head-on:** closest match to the proposed low aircraft / large quiet sky opening.
- **#5, Grim Reapers:** the clearest long progression from a tiny distant formation to a readable nose-on aircraft.
- **#2, 2010 Fleet Week:** a restrained sky-only approach before infrastructure supplies scale.
- **#18, H18 Mach Loop:** anticipation, sudden close crossing, and empty sky afterward.
- **#20, Pensacola:** distant beach approach, upward tracking, and a close underside reveal.

The bonus Darkstar clip is useful separately for rear tracking.

## Implications for our next storyboard

The strongest references suggest a sequence worth testing, rather than a settled design: hold a tiny aircraft below center in a large sky (#1/#3), let it become recognizable before accelerating the camera (#5), reveal the silhouette through a slight bank or upward viewpoint (#9/#14), then settle into a clean level profile (#10/#12/the bonus F-22). Keep the water horizon as a scale cue rather than giving it half the screen. The Mach Loop examples demonstrate why foreground/background separation helps apparent speed, but the SR-71 scene can obtain that separation through cloud layers and reflection instead of literal mountains.

For the next board, compare two timings using the same composition: a long approach followed by one abrupt near pass, and a shorter approach with a smoother retreat/rise. These are proposals derived from the references; the videos do not demonstrate the exact continuous nose-to-overhead-to-poster camera move we want.
