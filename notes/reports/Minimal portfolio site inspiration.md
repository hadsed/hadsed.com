# Borrow Bret Victor's palette, Karpathy's timeline

Two live sites cover most of the hadsed.com brief between them. **worrydream.com (Bret Victor)** already uses a deep navy field (`#10183a`) with pale blue-gray text, white bold links, gray labels hung in the left gutter, and no cards anywhere. **karpathy.ai** has the only verified dot-and-line experience timeline among admired researcher sites: a thin gray spine, small orbs ringed in the page color, and right-aligned dim date spans ([worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/); [karpathy.ai/style.css](https://karpathy.ai/style.css)). Around those two, a short list of sites supplies the remaining pieces. Shu Ding's shud.in shows how to make a plain left text menu that collapses gracefully on mobile. Frank Chimero proves a tinted dark ground plus two text brightnesses is enough personality. Paco Coursey has the cleanest one-time load stagger. Ink & Switch, Thinking Machines, and Transluce show how to make a research index feel product-aware through metadata rather than chrome. The obvious nearest neighbor, **brittanychiang.com**, matches the slate-navy palette and sticky left column almost exactly, but it is a heavily cloned public template, so it is a reference to diverge from rather than to copy ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)). The specific combination Had wants (a persistent left text menu plus an orb timeline on navy) did not turn up on any verified high-quality site. That makes it a point of distinction, and it ports well to a future research.appliedrealities.ai.

## Two sites already define the palette and the timeline

**worrydream.com** is the closest thing to the target's mood. Its inline CSS sets `body { background:#10183a; color:#ace; font: 13px/1.2 "Avenir" }`, uses `#fff` bold links with underline only on hover, `#999` for headings and labels, `#346` for 1px hairline rules, and reserves a single yellow (`.good { color:#ff0 }`) for standout items, all in a `564px` centered column ([worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/)). Its signature structural move is **year labels absolutely positioned in the left gutter** beside a list indented 70px, which turns the whole page into a reverse-chronological timeline without drawing one. Its one interaction is restrained: hovering a link pins a full-height preview image on the right edge. Had should borrow the three-tier text system (blue-tinted body, white names and links, warm gray metadata), the single-accent rule for featured items, and the hover preview as a way to give project panels imagery without boxes. He should not borrow the 13px body, which is too small for blog prose.

**karpathy.ai** supplies the timeline mechanics. Each `.entry.row` is a grid of a `.timespan` column (14px, right-aligned, `#bbb`), an `.ico` column with `border-left: 2px solid #cfcfcf`, and a description. The `.entry-dot` is a 10px circle filled `#cfcfcf` with a **2px border in the page background color**, so the line appears cut around each orb, like a bead on a string ([karpathy.ai/style.css](https://karpathy.ai/style.css)). Section labels are large lowercase words ("bio", "teaching", "pet projects"), and the bio opens with a deliberately absurd paragraph, so the personality lives in the copy. On navy, invert it: spine around `#3a4560`, orbs the same color, orb ring equal to the background, dates in a dim tabular column. Skip the image cards Karpathy uses for talks and projects.

A third dark reference rounds out the palette options. **nonint.com** uses a warm charcoal (`#1a1b1d`) with cream text (`#f0efe9`), Source Serif 4 at a fluid `clamp(16px, …, 22px)`, and a tiny letter-spaced monospace header row with a 1px rule. It shows how a serif body plus a mono "lab header" reads as research without any decoration ([nonint.com/site.css](https://nonint.com/site.css)).

## Designer sites teach the menu, the entry format, and the motion budget

**brittanychiang.com** is the most literal match: computed background `rgb(15,23,42)` (Tailwind slate-900), body `slate-400`, headings `slate-200`, Inter, a sticky left column holding name, subtitle, tagline, and an "About / Experience / Projects" text nav, with dated experience rows on the right ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)). Its best idea is the **`nav-indicator`**: a 1px line before each menu item that grows from `w-8` to `w-16` and brightens on hover or active section, disabled under reduced motion. Notably, she has no line-and-dot timeline; her line motif lives in the nav. The parts to avoid are the teal pill tags, the row-hover background cards, and the cursor-following blue spotlight, which are both the "modern chrome" Had is rejecting and the most recognizable marks of her widely forked template ([brittanychiang.com raw HTML](https://brittanychiang.com/)).

**Shu Ding (shud.in)** has the cleanest persistent left menu. It is a right-aligned list ("about / thoughts / projects / guestbook") set in an **italic serif while the body is sans**, separated from content by a thin vertical rule. The typeface contrast alone marks it as navigation. At 375px the menu becomes a horizontal row of the same italic links, and the vertical rule becomes a horizontal one, with no hamburger. The Thoughts page is a dated list joined to `2026.10.22`-style dates by faint dotted leaders, and the CSS uses view transitions with reduced-motion handling ([shud.in](https://shud.in); [shud.in/thoughts](https://shud.in/thoughts)). With only four pages, this is the right mobile pattern for hadsed.com.

**Frank Chimero (frankchimero.com)** shows a dark site that does not read as dark mode: background `rgb(61,67,64)` (a warm gray-green), text `#eee`, a dim variable for secondary lines, and Courier at 14px/21px with no keyframes at all. Every entry is a **bright title over a dim descriptor with a middot date** ("The Shape of Design ↗ / Book · 2012") ([frankchimero.com](https://frankchimero.com)). That two-line pair is the right grammar for the experience timeline's name and subtitle.

**Paco Coursey (paco.me)** has the canonical tasteful load animation: `[data-animate]{animation:.6s both enter}` with `opacity 0→1, translateY(10px)→0`, delayed by `--stagger × 0.12s`, and switched off under reduced motion ([paco.me](https://paco.me)). It runs once and gets out of the way. **Emil Kowalski**, the design engineer most associated with animation, keeps his own home page nearly motionless and features his essay "You Don't Need Animations" ([emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski)). Secondhand summaries of his guidance put control transitions at roughly 140 to 220ms and warn that long staggers feel slow ([skillselion summary](https://skillselion.com/skills/nexu-io/open-design/emilkowalski-motion)). Paco's 120ms step is at the slow end of that, so a shorter step suits a timeline with many rows.

Several sites contribute single early-web touches worth stealing. **Linus Lee (thesephist.com)** formats talks and roles as compact rows with abbreviated dates ("Cursor Compile, *Notational Intelligence* Jun '26") ([thesephist.com](https://thesephist.com)). **Derek Sivers (sive.rs)** writes his life as a prose timeline ending "(2026+): ?" ([sive.rs](https://sive.rs)). **Benji Taylor (benji.org)** puts an "Updated Aug 22, 2026" stamp at the top of his page ([benji.org](https://benji.org)). **Anthony Hobday** lists blog posts with word counts instead of dates or thumbnails ([anthonyhobday.com](https://anthonyhobday.com)). **Niki Tonsky** groups posts under year headers with short `M/D` dates ([tonsky.me](https://tonsky.me)). **Rauno Freiberg** replaces mailto with click-to-copy email and a one-word "Copied" confirmation ([rauno.me](https://rauno.me)).

## Lab sites make research look product-aware through metadata, not chrome

The labs that stay minimal are small and independent. The funded AI labs have mostly moved to product-marketing homepages, and their minimalism survives only in the rows of their research lists. That split is the key lesson for the later research.appliedrealities.ai: take the page grammar from indie labs and the row grammar from AI labs.

**Ink & Switch** organizes its homepage into "Research Areas", "Featured Work", "Production Software", "Lab Meta", and "Appearances". Each entry carries a **typed metadata line** ("Lab Notebook", "2019 Essay") and appearances use compressed mono date stamps like `2026APR` ([inkandswitch.com](https://www.inkandswitch.com/)). Having a "Production Software" section inside a research site is exactly the product-meets-lab balance Had wants. **Thinking Machines** puts products (Inkling, Tinker) and a named research series (Connectionism) side by side in one nav, and its blog is a **numbered, image-free list** of title, muted author line, and date, with a sidebar grouping links under "Models", "Tinker", and "Company" ([thinkingmachines.ai](https://thinkingmachines.ai/); [thinkingmachines.ai/blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/)). That grouped sidebar is the closest lab precedent for a left menu that scales from a personal site to a lab site.

**Transluce** prefixes each news item with a "Research · September 30, 2026" kicker ([transluce.org](https://transluce.org/)). **Goodfire** prints the full author list under each paper title ([goodfire.com](https://www.goodfire.com/)). **Physical Intelligence**, now at pi.website, makes the whole homepage a dated research feed with a single "New" label as its only emphasis ([pi.website](https://www.pi.website/)). **Sakana** contributes venue kickers like "NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight" and "Open source", though its homepage has otherwise become a card grid ([sakana.ai](https://sakana.ai/)). **Dynamicland** states media type and length plainly ("6-minute intro, 2024 — Video, 6 min") and labels its people section "Who did this." ([dynamicland.org](https://dynamicland.org/)).

Among one-person labs, **Andy Matuschak** groups projects by kind and era ("Projects: tools for thought") with true small caps (Alegreya Sans SC) for labels and a section called "Letters from the lab" ([andymatuschak.org](https://andymatuschak.org/)). **Omar Rizwan** uses open-ended ranges ("Jun 2015–") and one ⭐ to mark featured work ([omar.website](https://omar.website/)), a natural fit for the repo's existing `featured` flag. **Hundred Rabbits** runs its homepage as a monthly log, month heading then a few bullets of what shipped ([100r.co](https://100r.co/site/home.html)). **Folk Computer** is a bare DokuWiki whose credibility comes from a visible "last modified 2026/10/08" footer and named people ([folk.computer](https://folk.computer/)). Among researcher pages, **Jon Barron** marks featured papers with a tinted row instead of a card ([jonbarron.info](https://jonbarron.info)), and **jxmo.io** lists projects as "[2025] one-sentence claim", the densest card-free format found ([jxmo.io](https://jxmo.io)).

## The target layout is rare, and the galleries mostly don't help

Persistent left text navigation is unusual in this genre. Almost every verified ML researcher and lab site uses a top text nav or none; the nearest precedents are Victor's hanging gutter labels, Shu Ding's serif menu, Brittany Chiang's sticky column, and Thinking Machines' grouped blog sidebar. Line-and-dot timelines appear mostly in templates and junior portfolios. Karpathy's is the only one found on a widely admired site, and Brittany Chiang, the best-known dark left-nav site, has none ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com/); [karpathy.ai](https://karpathy.ai)). One caveat: the research found fewer than five strong sites that combine both features, so "rare" rests on a sample of a few dozen hand-checked sites and roughly 400 grepped portfolios, not on an exhaustive survey.

For further browsing, the developer-oriented directories beat the award galleries. **personalsit.es** lists about 1,148 hand-built sites with tag filters and a random-site link ([personalsit.es](https://personalsit.es/)). The **HN Personal Websites Directory** lists 162 sites from the January 2026 "Share your personal website" thread ([hnpwd.github.io](https://hnpwd.github.io/)). **httpster** exposes `/style/dark/`, `/style/minimal/`, `/style/typographic/`, and `/style/non-standard-navigation/` filters, the last being the likeliest place to find more left menus ([httpster.net](https://httpster.net/)). **minimal.gallery** has Personal and Portfolio tags but no dark filter ([minimal.gallery](https://minimal.gallery/)). godly.website and awwwards' "minimal" collection favor heavy motion and WebGL, so they are useful only as a list of what to avoid ([awwwards.com](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/minimal/)). In the HN thread, commenters praised plain hand-built sites and called a "slight page jump" distracting, while one three.js background's own author admitted it was not performant on some devices ([Ask HN, Jan 2026](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/46618714)). westegg.com, a "timeline of interesting projects", shows the failure mode: every one of its 46 entries uses `fadeInUp` ([westegg.com](https://westegg.com)).

## Recurring patterns and what to do with them

Across all four strands the same handful of moves recur. Memorable minimal sites tint the dark ground (navy, gray-green, warm charcoal) instead of using `#000` or `#121212`. They use exactly two or three text brightnesses and keep the brightest off pure white. They introduce sections with small labels instead of headings or boxes. They format entries as a title plus one dim line of metadata. They spend personality on one typographic decision and one interaction, never more. The concrete recommendations for hadsed.com follow.

| Area | Do this | Drawn from |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | Navy ground near `#10183a`–`#0f172a`; blue-tinted gray body; near-white for names and links; warm gray for dates; one accent reserved for `featured` items | worrydream, Frank Chimero |
| Left menu | Plain text links in a contrasting face (italic serif or small-caps mono) with a 1px rule or growing line for the active page; on mobile, the same links in a horizontal row with a horizontal rule, no hamburger | Shu Ding, Brittany Chiang's nav-indicator |
| Timeline | One `<ol>` with a single 1px `::before` spine; 7–10px orbs ringed in the background color; only the current role filled; dates right-aligned in tabular or mono figures ("Jun '26", "2024 — Now") | karpathy.ai, thesephist, mariana.design |
| Entries and panels | Kicker line (`TYPE · Mon 'YY`), bright title, one-line descriptor, muted line for collaborators, venue, or status (Shipped / Prototype / Paper) | Ink & Switch, Transluce, Goodfire, Frank Chimero |
| Blog | Image-free, year- or month-grouped list with short dates; optionally word counts | Thinking Machines, tonsky, Anthony Hobday |
| Motion | One short load stagger on About blocks (well under 100ms per step), the spine drawing once, hover color or line growth, cross-page `@view-transition`; everything off under `prefers-reduced-motion` | Paco, Shu Ding |
| Early-web personality | "Updated <date>" or "last modified" line, an open-ended final timeline row, click-to-copy email, a colophon | Benji Taylor, Folk, sive.rs, Rauno |
| Lab reuse | Grouped left-menu headings (e.g. Lab / Writing) and a named research series so the same shell becomes research.appliedrealities.ai | Thinking Machines, Matuschak |

The ideas to avoid are equally consistent. Do not clone Brittany Chiang's Inter-on-slate-900 look with pill tags, hover-lifted rows, and a cursor spotlight, because it reads as her template. Skip fade-ins on every list item, custom cursors, canvas or WebGL backgrounds, intro reveals like rauno.me's, and anything that shifts layout. Leave out thumbnails-as-cards (Karpathy's talks, Anthropic's carousel, Sakana's grid). Avoid themed academic blog templates such as PaperMod or al-folio, which read as generic. Do not stack quirks: pick one, such as Victor's hover preview or a single easter egg, and stop.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the brief: hadsed.com does not need a reference site to copy, because its distinguishing feature, an orb timeline under a plain left menu on navy, barely exists among admired sites. The work is assembly, not imitation. Victor's palette and gutter, Karpathy's bead-on-a-string orbs, Shu Ding's typographic menu, and Ink & Switch's typed metadata already fit together with almost no visual chrome. The main risk is gravitational pull toward brittanychiang.com, which shares the palette. A non-Inter typeface and the absence of her hover cards are enough to escape it.

The lab sites also suggest building the metadata system before the visual polish. Type kickers, status labels, author lines, and a featured marker are what make Ink & Switch and Thinking Machines read as both research and product. They are also what transfers when the same shell becomes research.appliedrealities.ai. A personal site that already speaks in "Prototype · Jun '26" and "Shipped" will turn into a lab index by swapping content, not redesigning.
